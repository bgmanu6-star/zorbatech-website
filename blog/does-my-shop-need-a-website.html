Does Your Shop Need a Website? An Honest Checklist

By Manu B · 15 November 2026 · 6 min read

Every week someone tells a shop owner they "must" have a website. Some of them are trying to sell one. I build websites for a living, and I'll still say this plainly: not every shop needs a full website on day one. What every shop does need is to be easy to find and easy to contact. Here's how to decide what's right for yours.

Start here: Google Business Profile (free)

When someone nearby searches "hardware shop near me", the first thing they see is usually the map with three shops listed under it. That list comes from Google Business Profile, not from websites. It's free, and for many local shops it brings more calls than a website ever will.

If you do only one thing this month, do this:

  1. Create or claim your shop at business.google.com.
  2. Use your real shop name, exact location pin, phone number and opening hours.
  3. Add 8 to 10 clear photos: the shop front (so people recognise it from the road), the counter, and your main products.
  4. Ask your regular customers for a review. Ten honest reviews beat a fancy website.

When a website actually pays off

A website starts earning its cost when one of these is true for you:

  • Customers ask the same questions on the phone all day. Prices, stock, brands you carry, delivery area. A simple page answers them while you serve the customer in front of you.
  • You sell to contractors, builders or other businesses. They often check you online before placing a big order. A proper site with your GSTIN, address and product range makes you look like a supplier they can trust.
  • You want orders from outside your street. A catalogue with a WhatsApp order button lets people order without calling.
  • You run a clinic, salon or restaurant. People want timings, menu or services, and a way to book, before they come.

If none of these apply yet, keep the Google profile up to date and come back to this list in six months.

What a small shop website should have

Skip the animations. These are the things customers actually use:

  • Works properly on a phone. Most of your visitors will be on mobile data, on a budget phone. Test it on one.
  • Tap-to-call and WhatsApp buttons at the top, not hidden at the bottom.
  • Your address with a Google Maps link and opening hours.
  • What you sell, with real photos, grouped the way customers think (for a hardware shop: cement and steel, paints, plumbing, electricals).
  • Your own domain like yourshop.in, registered in your name.
  • Fast loading. If it takes more than three seconds on 4G, people leave.

What it should cost, and the fees to watch for

A simple shop website is a one-time job. Your ongoing costs should be small and clear:

  • Domain name: usually somewhere between a few hundred and about fifteen hundred rupees a year for a .in or .com, paid to the registrar.
  • Hosting: for a simple static site, this can genuinely be free on services like GitHub Pages or Cloudflare.
Red flags: a monthly fee just to keep a simple site online; a developer who registers the domain in their own name; no access to your own site files; or a price that keeps changing after the work starts. Ask for a written quote with a fixed scope before paying anything.

A 10-minute self-check

Answer these honestly:

  1. If someone searches your shop's name on Google right now, do they find your correct phone number and timings?
  2. Do you get the same 5 questions on the phone every day?
  3. Do builders, offices or other shops buy from you?
  4. Would you take orders on WhatsApp if people could see your products?

If you said no to 1, fix your Google profile first. It's free. If you said yes to two or more of 2–4, a website will likely pay for itself.
